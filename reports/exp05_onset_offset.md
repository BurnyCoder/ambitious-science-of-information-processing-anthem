# Experiment 05: is the Suno–Whisper onset lag Suno's or Whisper's?

**Status: pre-registered.** This section was written and committed before any separation, alignment or comparison was run. Results are appended below it; nothing above the "Results" heading is edited after the run except typo fixes, which are marked.

## Background

[Exp03](exp03_alignment_repair.md) (post-hoc analysis) found that the word onsets the karaoke uses (Suno timings, repair v2) are later than faster-whisper onsets by a near-constant lag:

| Take | Matched words | Median signed offset (Suno − Whisper) | Share with Suno later | Spread around the lag (median \|d − median\|) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| take 1 (video) | 393 | +0.234 s | 98.5 % | 0.075 s |
| take 2 | 401 | +0.165 s | 93.8 % | 0.089 s |

Either Suno marks onsets late, or Whisper marks them early (Whisper's cross-attention word timestamps are known to be imprecise, which motivated forced-alignment add-ons such as WhisperX; Bain et al., 2023). A 0.23 s lag is 7 video frames, so if it is Suno's, every word in the video lights up visibly late. Exp03 could not decide this and deliberately applied no correction.

## Question and competing hypotheses

**Question.** Measured against a stronger reference that knows the lyrics and listens to the isolated voice, which system's onsets are biased, and by how much?

Let δ = median over lead words of (onset_Suno − onset_ref), where onset_ref is the stronger reference below.

| Hypothesis | Prediction for δ (take 1) | Prediction for Whisper − ref | It is falsified if |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H_S** (Suno is late) | ≈ +0.23 s, inside [+0.10, +0.35] s | ≈ 0 | the 95 % CI of δ lies entirely below +0.10 s |
| **H_W** (Whisper is early) | ≈ 0, inside [−0.05, +0.05] s | ≈ −0.23 s | the 95 % CI of δ lies entirely above +0.05 s |
| Mixed | between the two | between the two | (both of the above are falsified, which is consistent with it) |

The two hypotheses make predictions about 0.23 s apart, which is more than 6 standard errors even in the worst case of the power calculation below, so this design can distinguish them.

## Stronger reference (fixed in advance)

1. **Vocal separation.** Demucs `htdemucs` (Hybrid Transformer Demucs; Rouard, Massa & Défossez, 2022) separates each take's MP3 into drums, bass, other and vocals. Default inference settings: `split=True`, `overlap=0.25`, `shifts=1`, CPU. Only the vocal stem is used below. Stems are written to `out/exp05/` (gitignored; they are derived from the cached MP3 and regenerated by the script).
2. **Lyrics-aware forced alignment.** torchaudio's `MMS_FA` bundle (the Massively Multilingual Speech forced-alignment model; Pratap et al., 2023), applied to the vocal stem resampled to 16 kHz mono.
   - **Transcript.** All 430 written words of `content/lyrics.txt` in written order, lead and backing, normalised to the model's alphabet (lowercase a–z; our normalised keys contain no digits).
   - **Long audio.** Emissions are computed in 30 s windows with 2 s of context on each side, which is cropped before concatenation, following `ctc-forced-aligner` (Ashraf, 2024). CTC forced alignment (`torchaudio.functional.forced_align` via the bundle's aligner) is then run once over the whole song.
   - **Onset.** A word's onset is the first frame of its first character span, converted to seconds with the samples-per-frame ratio (20 ms frames).
   - **Why this reference.** It is given the lyrics (no recognition errors, every word gets a time) and it hears the voice without the orchestra. It is still a model trained on read speech, so its absolute timing on singing is validated separately (anchor check).
3. **Whisper.** Re-run exactly as in exp03 (faster-whisper `small.en`, CPU int8, beam 5, full mix), with written-to-recognised words matched by exact difflib blocks. This gives the third side of the triangle.

## Anchor check: hand-labelled acoustic onsets (fixed in advance)

The forced aligner could have its own constant bias (CTC posteriors are "peaky", which can misplace boundaries; Huang et al., 2024). A physical anchor that is independent of all three models: the moment the voice starts again after a rest.

- **Selection (symmetric, so it favours neither system).** Lines whose first word is a lead word and whose gap before that word (previous word's end → this word's start) is ≥ 0.8 s in **both** the Suno and the MMS timings. If more than 20 lines qualify, a random sample of 20 is drawn with `numpy.random.default_rng(0)`.
- **Panels.** For each selected line, a 1.6 s window of the vocal stem is plotted: log-mel spectrogram, RMS in dB, and the onset-strength envelope (librosa, 22.05 kHz, hop 256 = 11.6 ms, the hop validated in exp02). `librosa.onset.onset_detect(backtrack=True)` onsets are drawn as lettered lines (A, B, C, …). The panel title gives only the first word's text.
- **Blinding.** The window centre is the midpoint of the Suno and MMS onsets plus a hidden jitter drawn from U(−0.25, 0.25) s (seed 0). The time axis starts at 0 at the window start. Suno, MMS and Whisper onsets are **not** drawn, and the key mapping window to song time is written to a separate file that is not opened until all labels are written.
- **Labelling.** For each panel the annotator (the agent running this experiment, who knows the hypotheses but not the hidden positions) records which lettered onset is the start of the line's first word, or a manual time if the detector missed it, or `ambiguous` (e.g. choir or reverb masking the start). Labels are committed in `reports/data/exp05_anchor_labels.json` before the comparison phase runs. Ambiguous panels are excluded.
- **Anchor metrics.** Over the usable anchors: α_S = median(Suno − anchor), β = median(MMS − anchor), α_W = median(Whisper − anchor) on the anchors Whisper also matched, each with a 95 % percentile-bootstrap CI over anchors (10 000 resamples, seed 0).

## Primary metric and analysis plan

- **Primary metric.** δ (take 1, the take used in the video): median of onset_Suno(repair v2, `anthem.alignment.align_words`) − onset_MMS over the 405 lead words. Backing words (in parentheses; 25 words) are excluded because they overlap the lead voice.
- **Uncertainty.** 95 % CI by a **line-cluster** percentile bootstrap: lines (56 lead lines) are resampled with replacement and all their words kept, 10 000 resamples, seed 0. Words within a line are not independent, so a word-level bootstrap would be too narrow; it is reported as a sensitivity analysis.
- **Power.** From exp03's spread (median |d − median| = 0.075 s, i.e. σ ≈ 1.4826 · 0.075 = 0.11 s), the standard error of a median is ≈ 1.2533 σ / √n: 0.0069 s with n = 405 words, and 0.019 s in the worst case where each of the 56 lines counts only once. The worst-case 95 % CI half-width is therefore ≈ 0.036 s, about one video frame, against a 0.23 s separation between H_S and H_W.
- **Secondary (descriptive, no decision attached).**
  1. The same δ for take 2 (replication on the second take).
  2. Whisper − MMS for both takes, signed median with the same line-cluster CI.
  3. Median \|Suno − MMS\| and \|Whisper − MMS\| with CIs, plus the random-shift control of exp03: U(−1, 1) s added to the Suno onsets must raise the median \|Δ\|.
  4. Residual spread around the lag, median \|d − δ\|, and δ per section, to see whether the lag is constant over the song. A global offset is only a good model if it is.
  5. Sensitivity of δ to (a) the word-level bootstrap and (b) only words whose mean MMS token probability is ≥ 0.5.
- **Technical checks.**
  1. *Stem timebase.* The cross-correlation lag between the mix and the sum of the four stems (first 60 s, mono) must be 0 samples. Otherwise the stem is shifted and everything above is invalid.
  2. *Playback timebase.* The cross-correlation lag between the take MP3 as decoded by librosa and the audio track of the rendered `out/anthem.mp4` (decoded by ffmpeg). A non-zero lag would shift the karaoke in the video regardless of the word times; it is reported, not corrected.

## Decision rule (fixed in advance)

The video's word times get a global offset `VIDEO_WORD_OFFSET_S = −δ` (rounded to 1 ms) if and only if all of these hold:

- **D1.** The 95 % line-cluster CI of δ excludes 0.
- **D2.** \|δ\| ≥ 0.033 s, one video frame at 30 fps.
- **D3. The reference is validated.** At least 10 usable anchors, and \|β\| ≤ 0.05 s (MMS agrees with the hand-labelled onsets to within 1.5 frames in median).
- **D4. The anchors agree on the direction.** α_S has the same sign as δ and its 95 % CI excludes 0.

Fallback if D3 fails but D4 holds and \|α_S\| ≥ 0.033 s: the offset is −α_S (anchor-based) instead, reported as weaker evidence because the anchors cover line starts only. In every other case the offset stays 0 and the report says which condition failed.

If an offset is applied, it is implemented as a `.env` setting (`VIDEO_WORD_OFFSET_S`, default 0) added to every word's start and end in `anthem/timeline.py`, so section cuts (which follow each section's first word) move with it. It is calibrated on take 1. It is not validated for take 2.

## Known limitations, stated in advance

- One song, one generation, two takes. Nothing here is a claim about Suno in general.
- Demucs vocals contain the choir and backing vocals as well as the lead.
- MMS was trained on speech, not singing. The anchor check tests its absolute timing only at line starts after a rest, which may differ from mid-line words.
- There is one annotator, who is not blind to the hypotheses. Hiding the candidate positions and jittering the window limits but does not remove that bias.
- "Onset" means the start of the first sound of the word (the consonant, if any). Where a karaoke highlight should land perceptually, on the consonant or the vowel, is not tested.

## Amendment 1: anchor selection (decided blind, before any comparison)

**What happened.** Run 1 computed all three references and applied the anchor selection rule. Only **1** of the 56 lead line starts of take 1 qualified, against the pre-registered target of about 20 and the D3 minimum of 10.

**Why.** We diagnosed this from each system's own timings, without computing any Suno-vs-MMS, Whisper or anchor difference:

| Gap before a lead line start (previous word's end → first word's start) | min | 25 % | median | 75 % | max | ≥ 0.8 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Suno (repair v2) | 0.004 s | 0.040 s | 0.060 s | 0.073 s | 7.415 s | 1 |
| MMS_FA | 0.000 s | 0.055 s | 0.220 s | 0.420 s | 7.100 s | 9 |

Suno's word spans are nearly contiguous: a line-final word runs on until the next line starts (median duration 0.74 s). Suno's "gap" is therefore not a measure of a vocal rest, and the rule "≥ 0.8 s in both timings" could almost never hold. This was a design error in the selection rule, not a property of the singing.

**Change.** The rest criterion and the subsampling are dropped. *All 56 lead line starts* of take 1 are panelled. The selection now uses only the lyric structure (a lead word that starts a line), so it depends on no system's timing at all. Lines whose start is not clearly visible (legato joins, vowel-after-vowel, choir masking) are marked `ambiguous` by the blind annotator, exactly as pre-registered. Panel titles also show the previous word and the first three words of the line, so the annotator can find the word boundary when there is no rest. Window, jitter, blinding, labels and all decision conditions are unchanged.

**Status.** This amendment was committed before the comparison phase was run, and nothing was computed that compares two systems' onsets. Under the rule as originally written, D3 fails (1 < 10 anchors). The fallback also needs anchors, so the original rule would have produced "offset 0" for a purely mechanical reason. Both outcomes are reported below; the amended one is operative.

**Decision (pre-registered rule, amendment 1).**

| Condition | Value | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| D1: CI of δ excludes 0 | [0.125, 0.147] | yes |
| D2: \|δ\| ≥ 0.033 s | 0.137 s | yes |
| D3: ≥ 10 usable anchors and \|β\| ≤ 0.05 s | 34 anchors, β = 0.041 s | yes |
| D4: α_S has δ's sign and its CI excludes 0 | +0.220 s [0.148, 0.347] | yes |

→ **`VIDEO_WORD_OFFSET_S = −0.137`** (basis: MMS). Under the anchor rule as originally pre-registered, only 1 anchor qualified, D3 failed, and the offset would have stayed 0 (see Amendment 1).

**Hypotheses.** H_W ("Whisper is early, Suno is right") is **falsified**: the CI of δ, [0.125, 0.147], lies entirely above +0.05 s. H_S ("Suno is late") is **not falsified**: the CI does not lie below +0.10 s. But the effect is smaller than H_S predicted (0.137 vs about 0.23 s), because Whisper is also biased early by 0.08 s. The 0.23 s lag of exp03 is therefore shared: about 0.14 s is Suno being late and about 0.08 s is Whisper being early, both measured against MMS.

### Post-hoc analyses (not pre-registered)

- **The correction, checked on the anchors.** δ was estimated from MMS alone, so the hand-labelled anchors are an independent test of it.
  - After shifting by −0.137 s, the median \|Suno − anchor\| fell from 0.233 s [0.177, 0.347] to 0.131 s [0.049, 0.235].
  - 32 of 34 anchors (94 %) moved closer; one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank p = 9 × 10⁻⁷.
  - On the 18 high-confidence anchors it fell from 0.187 s to 0.051 s.
  - At line starts the corrected onsets are still late by +0.083 s [0.011, 0.210]. Two things contribute: Suno is later at line starts (α_S = 0.220 s) than over all words (δ + β ≈ 0.18 s), and MMS itself is about 0.04 s late.
- **Whisper is earliest at line starts.** Whisper − MMS is −0.08 s over all words, but Whisper − anchor is −0.29 s at line starts. This fits the known tendency of Whisper's timestamps to absorb the preceding pause into the first word after it (Bain et al., 2023).
- **Possible label errors.** Five anchors have both Suno and MMS more than 0.3 s late (panels 19, 21, 49, 43, 2). The likeliest explanation is that the annotator picked an earlier syllable. Errors in that direction make both α_S and β look *later*, so β = 0.041 s is, if anything, an overestimate of MMS's bias. The medians are robust to five outliers out of 34.

## Interpretation and decision

- **What we conclude.** On take 1, Suno's word onsets are late, by 0.137 s against a forced alignment of the known lyrics on the separated voice. The lag is constant over the song (no drift, section medians 0.11–0.16 s, residual spread 0.046 s). The forced aligner agrees with hand-labelled acoustic onsets to within 0.04 s.
- **What changed in the video.** The pre-registered rule applied a global offset of −0.137 s to every word. Section cuts follow, but they stay snapped to beats: only the bridge cut moved, from 164.502 s to the previous beat at 164.049 s. The video was re-rendered and verified (exp04's criteria; see the README for the release).
- **What we do not conclude.**
  - The offset is **take-specific**: take 2's lag is 0.061 s, while Whisper's bias against MMS is the same −0.08 s on both takes. So the lag lives in Suno's per-take alignment, not in a fixed pipeline delay, and −0.137 s must not be reused for another take or song without measuring it.
  - Absolute accuracy better than about one frame is not claimed. MMS's own +0.04 s (CI 0.009–0.105 s) means the corrected karaoke is probably still about one frame late on average, and more at line starts.
- **Remaining limitations.**
  - The anchors were labelled by the agent from plots, not by listening.
  - 22 of 56 panels were ambiguous.
  - The amendment changed the anchor sample after run 1. It was made before any comparison, but it was still made after the original rule failed.
